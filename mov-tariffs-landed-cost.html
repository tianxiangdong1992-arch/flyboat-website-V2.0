How Do Tariffs Affect the Landed Cost of Imported MOVs?

Two quotes for the same varistor can land at very different totals. Five checks that decide the real delivered cost — and how to compare quotes on an equal basis.

The short answer

The listing or EXW price of an imported MOV is only one part of landed cost. Duties, freight, insurance, customs brokerage and domestic handling are added on top — and duty treatment depends on the product's HS classification and the tariff programs in force at the time of entry, so two quotes for the same part can land at very different totals.

How to compare quotes on an equal basis

  • Fix the Incoterm first: EXW, FOB and DDP quotes are not comparable. Convert everything to one delivered basis before comparing unit prices.
  • Confirm the HS classification: duty exposure follows the classification of the actual product; ask the seller which classification their quote assumes.
  • Separate the duty line: a transparent quote shows product, freight and duty treatment as separate lines. A single “all-in” number hides which part moves when policy changes.
  • Ask who is importer of record: that party carries customs compliance and duty liability. A North American seller quoting on a delivered basis absorbs that complexity for the buyer.
  • Re-check at order time: tariff programs change; a landed cost calculated last quarter is not a safe basis for this quarter's order.
