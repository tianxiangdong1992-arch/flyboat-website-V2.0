Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to the questions buyers ask us most — certifications and how to verify them, replacement checks, manufacturing, testing, capacity, lead times, sourcing checks, OEM programs and SPDs.

What certifications do your MOVs carry, which models are covered, and how can I verify them?

Our metal oxide varistors are UL 1449 Recognized under File E335803 (UL 1449 and CSA C22.2 No. 269.5-17, Type 5 component recognition), held by Guiyang High-Tech Yige Electronic Co., Ltd. You can verify the file yourself in UL Product iQ (category VZCA2 / VZCA8, file E335803).

How to read our model codes: the three digits give the varistor voltage — the first two digits, followed by as many zeros as the third digit says. For example, code 431 = 430 V varistor voltage, and code 122 = 1,200 V. (The maximum continuous working voltage is a separate, lower datasheet value — for the 431K it is 275 V AC / 350 V DC.)

Coverage by series (voltages below are varistor voltages):

  • 34S series: 31 model grades covered, varistor voltages from 82 V to 1,800 V (codes 820 and 101–182); the 430–680 V grades (codes 431–681) are rated Imax 50 kA
  • 22S / 20S series: 22 model grades covered, varistor voltages from 100 V to 820 V (codes 101–821)
  • 27L series: 23 model grades covered, varistor voltages from 82 V to 820 V (codes 820 and 101–821)
  • 30S series: five grades covered — 620 V, 680 V, 820 V, 1,100 V and 1,200 V (codes 621, 681, 821, 112, 122)
  • DC models: the file also recognizes DC-rated variants of the series above, including DC-only grades in 22S and 30S and one 54L model, 54LK911 DC.
  • Series 25D, 30D, 32D, 40D, 44L, 48L are not UL covered — we state this openly so your compliance team does not have to guess.

For series not yet UL covered, we can pursue UL certification for your program — talk to us early in the design-in process so certification is planned into the program from the start.

We also hold ISO 9001, ISO 14001 and ISO 45001 certification, and selected products carry TÜV certification. See our Certifications page for the full coverage table.

Do you manufacture your own varistor discs?

Yes. The discs are made in our own plant in Guiyang — pressing, sintering, metallization, coating and electrical testing are all done in-house, not outsourced or bought in as finished discs. This is why we can hold tolerances and surge ratings consistent from batch to batch, and why we can offer custom voltage grades and sizes.

Can you provide datasheets and test reports?

Yes. Every series has a full datasheet (electrical parameters, surge ratings, dimensions). Type-test and routine-test reports are available for qualified OEM programs, and our in-house laboratory is equipped with 120 kA 8/20 µs and 15 kA 10/350 µs lightning surge testers, a 2 ms rectangular surge tester and a power-frequency temporary overvoltage (TOV) tester, so verification testing can be done on the same equipment class used in production release.

What should I check when replacing a thermally protected MOV such as the Littelfuse TMOV34S series?

There is no drop-in equivalent. Flyboat MOVs are not thermally protected and are not a direct replacement for TMOV-type parts, which combine a varistor with a thermal fuse element and status monitoring. If a design relies on those functions, thermal disconnection has to be provided at the module or equipment level — for example with a separate disconnector — and the design re-evaluated.

For any MOV replacement or second source, compare at least: maximum continuous operating voltage (AC and DC), varistor voltage and tolerance, clamping voltage at the specified current, energy rating and peak current (8/20 µs), disc diameter and lead spacing, and the UL recognition status of the exact model. Our 34S series uses the same 34 mm format as TMOV34S-type parts, and our North American engineering team can run a parameter-by-parameter comparison and provide samples for evaluation.

What is the difference between UL Recognized and UL Listed for MOVs used in SPDs?

UL Listed applies to complete end products evaluated for their intended installation. UL Recognized applies to components — such as MOVs — that are evaluated for use inside an end product under stated Conditions of Acceptability, and are taken into account when the end product is certified. MOVs are components, so component recognition is the normal route: Flyboat MOVs are UL 1449 Recognized under File E335803 (category VZCA2 for the US / VZCA8 for Canada, Type 5 component recognition). When a supplier quotes a UL file for a varistor, check in UL Product iQ whether it is a Recognition record and which exact models it covers.

What should I check before buying MOVs from Alibaba or other online marketplaces?

Marketplace listings are not an authorized distribution channel, and the ratings shown in listings are not always accurate. Before buying, check at least four things: (1) ask for the UL file number and verify it yourself in UL Product iQ — check that the certificate holder name matches the actual manufacturer and that the exact model is covered; (2) ask whether the seller is the manufacturer or an authorized channel, and how production lots are traced; (3) require a Certificate of Conformance with batch identification for each shipment; (4) compare ratings against the manufacturer's own datasheet, not the listing — surge ratings in particular are sometimes overstated.

Flyboat sells to North America through its own sales and engineering center: every quote identifies the UL file (E335803) and the exact models it covers, and each shipment carries a Certificate of Conformance traceable to its production batch.

How do tariffs and duties affect the landed cost of imported MOVs?

The landed cost of an imported MOV order is more than the unit price: it also includes international freight and insurance, US customs duties under the applicable tariff classification (varistors are generally classified under HS 8533.40), any additional duties that apply based on the product's country of origin, plus brokerage and local delivery. Who pays the duties depends on the trade terms — under EXW or FOB terms the buyer bears them; under delivered terms they are included in the seller's price. When comparing quotes, ask each supplier to state the trade terms and exactly what the price includes. Our team quotes with the fulfillment terms stated up front, so the landed cost is clear before you order.
