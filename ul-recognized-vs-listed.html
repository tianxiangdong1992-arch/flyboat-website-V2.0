UL Recognized vs UL Listed: What Is the Difference?

For MOVs and SPDs the two marks certify different things — complete end products versus components. Here is how to tell them apart, and how to verify any supplier's UL claim yourself in UL Product iQ.

The short answer

UL Listed applies to complete end products evaluated for their intended installation. UL Recognized applies to components that are evaluated for use inside an end product, under stated Conditions of Acceptability. A metal oxide varistor (MOV) is a component, so the correct UL route for a varistor is component recognition under UL 1449 — not listing. If a supplier advertises a “UL Listed” varistor, that wording alone is a reason to ask for the file number and check what the record actually says.

What UL Listed means

Listing is UL's evaluation of a complete product for its intended use and installation. A listed product is tested as a whole device — enclosure, connections, protective functions and ratings together — and may carry the UL Listing mark when it is installed as evaluated.

A finished surge protective device is an end product: panel-mount and DIN-rail SPDs are listed devices under UL 1449 (for example Type 1 and Type 2 SPDs), because the safety case — enclosure, disconnection, fusing, follow current and ratings — only exists at the level of the complete device.

What UL Recognized means

Component Recognition is UL's evaluation of a component that is intended to be built into an end product. The component is evaluated under stated Conditions of Acceptability — ratings and application limits the end-product designer must respect — and it is taken into account when the finished end product is certified.

For surge-protective components, recognition is granted under UL 1449 as Type 5 component recognition, and the public records appear in UL Product iQ under category VZCA2 (United States) and VZCA8 (Canada). Recognized components carry the UL Recognized Component mark — a different mark from the Listing mark, for a different job.

Why a varistor is Recognized, not Listed

An MOV has no standalone installation: it is soldered or assembled into an SPD, and the finished device provides the enclosure, the disconnector and the thermal protection that complete the safety case. UL therefore recognizes the MOV as a component and lists the finished SPD. When a varistor datasheet says “UL Listed”, it is mixing the two categories — what the supplier may actually hold (if anything) is a Recognition record. The wording matters because a compliance review will check the record, not the datasheet: ask for the file number and verify the record type, the holder and the exact models covered.

How to check a supplier's UL claim

  • Ask for the UL file number, then search it yourself in UL Product iQ (productiq.ulprospector.com) — do not rely on a logo printed on a datasheet.
  • Check the record type: for a varistor you should find a Component Recognition record, not a Listing.
  • Check the certificate holder: the name on the record should be the actual manufacturer — not a trader, and not an unrelated company.
  • Check the exact models covered: recognition applies model by model. A file that covers one series or voltage range does not cover another.
  • Read the ratings in the record — varistor voltage range, surge ratings and the Conditions of Acceptability. Those are the values that count in a compliance review, not marketing numbers.
